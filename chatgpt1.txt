2. Three additional opportunities that could make FlexPay stronger
A. Employer-to-merchant network
Instead of onboarding employees one by one, FlexPay can onboard an employer, allocate benefits to its employees, and connect them to participating merchants.

Employers get a repeatable employee-benefit service.

B. Merchant-funded PayLater as an alternative
Your research notes identify an interesting alternative to bank-funded credit: a large merchant could choose to provide trade credit to verified employees, while smaller merchants could require immediate settlement.

This creates two possible models:

Merchant-funded: A participating retailer agrees to wait for repayment under a commercial agreement.

Partner-funded: A suitable licensed financial partner funds eligible purchases and carries the agreed credit risk.

Opportunity: FlexPay could support both models through one checkout engine. However, for the hackathon, present this as a future capability—not a feature that already exists.

C. Refunds and reconciliation across multiple balances
Suppose a 2,000 ETB purchase is split between 1,500 ETB in employer benefits and 500 ETB in PayLater. The customer returns the purchase.

FlexPay must correctly reverse the original allocations, update the benefit and outstanding repayment balances, and reconcile the transaction with the payment provider and financing partner.

Opportunity: Demonstrate a complete refund flow. This is a strong technical differentiator because it proves the split-payment model can handle real transaction lifecycle events, not only successful purchases.

4. The coordination rules that prevent the agents from fighting
Use these rules throughout the build.

One repository, one source of truth

One owner per area

Gemini owns research and setup; Opus owns backend transaction integrity; Sonnet owns the frontend and browser tests. Any cross-owner change must be coordinated.

No false payment success

A simulation, an accepted payment request, a pending callback, and a confirmed provider transaction are four different things. The UI must show the actual state.

Test before adding features
